# Carrier Programme — 39-Repository Missed-Evidence Audit

Date: 18 July 2026  
Question: what existed outside the repositories used during the three-day Carrier programme that could have improved experiment design or interpretation?  
Scope: all 39 repositories owned by `StoneMonkeMatt`, public and private

## Direct answer

The missed repositories contained useful evidence. The most important was the complete `Observer-Fix` emergence-history sequence. It would probably have changed the order of the higher-order experiments by placing persistence-transition projection ahead of, or alongside, duality acceleration.

The missed evidence does not show that the Carrier numerical results are false. It shows that the architecture audit used to choose the sequence of experiments was narrower than the available account history.

## Corpus coverage

- 39 repositories inventoried: 22 private and 17 public.
- 38 repositories mirrored with complete objects and refs.
- `openclaw`, the largest unrelated repository, inspected through a metadata-first mirror covering 16,705 commits; no Carrier-related path or commit-message match was found.
- 20,217 reachable commits inspected across the 38 external repository histories, including the metadata-first `openclaw` history.
- Searches covered paths, commit messages, exact cited hashes, exact experiment identifiers, and the source terms `observer`, `emergence`, `persistence`, `threshold`, `duality`, `telos`, `compass`, `codex`, `mod97`, `mathematics`, and `carrier`.

Six commits dated 2–3 April 2026 preserve an unusually clear design sequence:

| Commit | Change | Experimental implication |

| `2b3e174` | Renamed the state to high-duality persistence and added `highDualityPersistenceSteps=2` | Separates categorical observer language from measurable persistence state |

This history gives persistence-transition direction a stronger native provenance than pure acceleration. It also supplies the precise state machine needed to avoid inventing persistence semantics during candidate projection.

## What should have changed three days ago

### Experiment ordering

After Carrier-003D closed fixed-weight slope usefulness, the account history supported this order more strongly:

1. dark projection of the complete high-duality predicate;
2. candidate projection of persistence transition/reset;
3. native threshold-boundary transition;
4. acceleration as a lower-priority continuous-history comparison.

The actual sequence placed acceleration first. That was mathematically coherent from the visible `dHist` interface, but it was not the strongest choice once the private `Observer-Fix` lineage is considered.

### Preregistration detail

The private sources would have supplied exact definitions for:

- integer persistence count;
- minimum value of one;
- reset on predicate failure;
- separation of diagnostic pass from final declaration;
- six predicate components;
- historical difference between threshold latch and compound persistence.

This reduces the amount of new semantics needed for a persistence experiment.

### Interpretation

The private chronology would have prevented these overstatements:

- treating public-only inspection as account-wide archaeology;
- presenting the invented `EXP-001`–`EXP-008` labels as recovered identifiers;
- implying that level, slope, and acceleration exhaust the most native historical controls;
- describing boundary crossing alone as the complete Needle mechanism.

## Were the three days wasted?

No, but some sequencing time was probably avoidable.

Carrier-003B through Carrier-003F still establish real facts about the frozen sequence engine: reachability, chronology sensitivity, sham robustness, and lack of demonstrated usefulness for the tested fixed-weight level/slope/acceleration forms. Those results remain reusable negative and mechanistic evidence.

The avoidable part was exhausting acceleration before inspecting the explicit persistence lineage. A persistence-transition experiment should now be treated as the next strongest native audit, informed directly by `Observer-Fix` rather than invented from the current run loop alone.

## Recommended correction to the programme

1. Preserve all completed Carrier results and receipts.
2. Add the `Observer-Fix` chronology to the mathematics ledger.
3. Reclassify persistence-transition projection as the strongest next candidate.
4. Design its dark audit from the exact historical diagnostic and reset semantics.
5. Keep threshold-only transition as a separate, narrower signal.
6. Do not use the unsupported `EXP-001`–`EXP-008` labels as historical identifiers.
